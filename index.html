Steered paraphrase pretraining: what changes, and where the gains come from

Small LMs pretrained on FAQ paraphrases generated with activation steering (register or wiki direction). Results summary; every number below is computed from the CSVs in data/.

SummarySetup1 Benchmarks2 Held-out loss3 Loss sources4 Answers vs context5 Training answers6 Span preference7 Shown / not shown

Summary

  1. Benchmarks: macro-score changes range from +0.01 (not significant, Llama wiki α35) to +1.03 points; only Qwen2 1.7B register α35 is clearly above seed noise. Register leads wiki, by margins within seed noise.
  2. Language modelling: every steered model lowers held-out loss by ~1–2% perplexity, wiki most and α35 least: the reverse of the benchmark ranking.
  3. Mechanism (loss): gains on hard/rare tokens and layout; α35 gives up gains on function words and prose.
  4. Mechanism (benchmarks): gains are on the gold answer, not on reading the context. Steered models prefer short span answers, most at α35, and this roughly tracks the benchmark gains.
  5. Data: steering makes FAQ answers open more directly and contain more numbers that are not in the source document; register and wiki data are nearly identical. What separates register from wiki is not known.

Setup

  • Models: Llama 1B and Qwen2 1.7B, pretrained from scratch, one seed each. Each architecture has a base model and four steered models.
  • Data: one epoch over a 20B-token mix, 50:50 synthetic and human (FineWeb). The synthetic half is FineWeb rewritten as FAQs by SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct. Base: no steering. Register and wiki: a steering vector added at layer 14 with strength α = 15 or 35.
  • Units: benchmark Δ in points (prob_norm_token × 100); loss Δ in centi-nats (nats × 100), positive = better than base. CIs are 95% paired bootstraps.

1 · Benchmarks: small gains, mostly within seed noise

Paper-style evaluation (lighteval, 3-shot, cloze formulation, prob_norm_token), every question of every task, paired bootstrap against each architecture's base model. Bars show the change in the macro score with its 95% CI; the shaded band is the seed-to-seed standard deviation of this score reported by the finephrase paper (0.16–0.49 points). One seed per model.

6 · Steered models prefer to answer with a short span

On 2,500 benchmark items (500 per QA task), the probability that the first token after Answer: starts a span (a number, name or word) rather than a sentence ("The", "It", "Yes", …), and the models' own greedy answers. 0-shot = question with its passage/table only; 3-shot = the benchmark prompt.

0-shot (question only)3-shot (benchmark prompt)
  • Every steered model prefers spans more than its base, in both settings (31 of 32 differences significant, counting span probability and greedy answers). Without demos, base models answer with a sentence 89–91% of the time; α35 models 74–79%.
  • Register > wiki in 4 of 4 comparisons at 0-shot, but only 1 of 4 at 3-shot.
  • The preference roughly tracks the benchmark gains across the 8 models (Spearman ρ: P(span) 0.81 at 0-shot, 0.67 at 3-shot; greedy span answers 0.57 / 0.43). Llama wiki α35 is the clearest exception at 0-shot (second-largest span shift among Llama models, smallest Llama gain).
  • Greedy answers do not contain the gold answer more often (15–19% for every model): what changes is the form of the answer and the probability of the gold span.

Caveats

  • One training seed per model. CIs on this page cover evaluation/item noise only.
  • α35 mixes also contain different human text (other CommonCrawl dumps, because longer synthetic documents shift which FineWeb shards fill the human half); this is collinear with α. Its measured effect on predictability is ~10× smaller than the synthetic difference, but it is not ruled out.
  • Source-document faithfulness concerns a document the model never sees during training.
