Steered paraphrase pretraining: what changes, and where the gains come from

Small LMs pretrained on FAQ paraphrases generated with activation steering (register or wiki direction). Results summary; every number below is computed from the CSVs in data/.

SummarySetup1 Benchmarks2 Held-out loss3 Loss sources4 Answers vs context5 Training answers6 Span preference7 Vectors8 Generation speed9 Shown / not shown

Summary

  1. Benchmarks: macro changes from +0.01 to +1.03 points (Qwen2 1.7B register α35; Qwen2 2.9B register α35 +0.71). Register leads wiki in all five comparisons. Gains on DROP, tables, XCSQA, GSM8K (Qwen); costs on LAMBADA and SQuADv2.
  2. Probability vs accuracy: at 1–1.7B the gain is mostly higher probability on answer-like text; at 2.9B it is more correct choices. Knowledge is unchanged.
  3. Language modelling: every steered model lowers held-out loss by ~1–2% perplexity, wiki most and α35 least: the reverse of the benchmark ranking.
  4. Mechanism (loss): gains on hard/rare tokens and layout; α35 gives up gains on function words and prose.
  5. Mechanism (benchmarks): gains are on the gold answer, not on reading the context. Steered models prefer short span answers, most at α35, and this roughly tracks the benchmark gains.
  6. Data: steering makes FAQ answers open more directly and contain more numbers that are not in the source document; register and wiki data are nearly identical.
  7. Vectors: more human sources give the same direction (cos 0.91–0.92 with register) with more cost. New contrasts are new directions; random directions do nothing to the text; register + a faithfulness correction removes the unsupported numbers while keeping the NLL shift.
  8. Generation: 3 vLLM engines per GPU, ~1.2× faster with the same output distribution.

Setup

  • Models: Llama 1B and Qwen2 1.7B (base + four steered models each) and Qwen2 2.9B (base, register α35, wiki α35), pretrained from scratch, one seed each.
  • Data: one epoch over a 20B-token mix, 50:50 synthetic and human (FineWeb). The synthetic half is FineWeb rewritten as FAQs by SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct. Base: no steering. Register and wiki: a steering vector added at layer 14 with strength α = 15 or 35.
  • Units: benchmark Δ in points (prob_norm_token × 100); loss Δ in centi-nats (nats × 100), positive = better than base. CIs are 95% paired bootstraps.

Probability vs accuracy: what the models actually learn

Only the 8 multiple-choice tasks have an accuracy (acc_norm_token). The two metrics answer different questions: probability = how much mass the correct answer gets; accuracy = whether it beats the wrong options. Probability up with accuracy flat means the wrong options rose too (a general boost to answer-like text). Accuracy up with probability flat means better choices without more confidence.

On 2,500 benchmark items (500 per QA task), the probability that the first token after Answer: starts a span (a number, name or word) rather than a sentence ("The", "It", "Yes", …), and the models' own greedy answers. 0-shot = question with its passage/table only; 3-shot = the benchmark prompt.

7 · Steering vectors: more sources, new contrasts

Same contrast, more sources. 16 human sources written before LLM text (Pile: wiki, gutenberg, freelaw, arxiv, pubmed, hackernews, enron, stackexchange; CC-News; Reddit; 6 CORE registers), 4,000 windows of 200–800 tokens each. Each window was paraphrased by SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct (neutral prompt, best of 10 samples; length ratio 0.85–1.18, ≤ 50% 5-gram overlap, no prompt echo), ≤ 2,000 pairs per source. Vector = whitened mean difference (human − paraphrase) of mean-pooled layer-14 activations, unit norm, the same construction as register. Split-half reliability 0.986–0.997; per-source cosine with register 0.45 (gutenberg) to 0.88 (wiki). register and wiki are the existing vectors from the earlier pipeline (used for all trained models); the composites average the new per-source vectors (equal weight per source):

How α was chosen: same-contrast composites and random controls use α35, like register (all vectors are unit norm). New contrasts use α = 35 × (spread of activations along the new direction ÷ spread along register), measured on SmolLM2 paraphrase activations at layer 14, so each pushes as far as register relative to natural variation. It's a rough yardstick: formulaicity at α15.3 shifts NLL almost twice as much as register. reg_plus_faith uses α39.1, which keeps its push along register at 35.
An α sweep (formulaicity α8/α11, edu α25/α45) is running to pick the final α by effect size.

  • The screen reproduces the corpora: reg35_regen matches the register α35 corpus on every measure.
  • The direction matters: random directions at the same norm barely change the text (-0.03 NLL vs +0.23).
  • More sources of the same contrast = register with more cost: all_mean_unit / no_outliers / expository give register's profile with longer outputs (+200 to +214 vs +134 tokens), more cap hits and more unsupported numbers.
  • reg_plus_faith keeps register's NLL shift and short openings but brings unsupported numbers back to base (-0.6 vs +20.6 points) and removes the length inflation; price: -12.4 points of FAQ-shaped documents.
  • formulaicity gives the largest NLL shift with fewer unsupported numbers than base and much less question-restating, but almost no short-opening effect: it separates "diversity" from register's answer style.
  • edu_quality raises the outputs' edu score (+0.40; register lowers it). temperature overshoots and costs faithfulness; answer_style is weak at its α.

8 · Faster corpus generation

Generation (vLLM, SmolLM2-1.7B on GH200) now runs 3 engines per GPU (memory share 0.30 each, 768 concurrent sequences, suffix speculative decoding) instead of one large engine. A sweep of serving settings found this the fastest: 1.19× (steered) and 1.24× (unsteered) more output per GPU. A 120k-document test reproduced it: 7,750 vs 6,681 output tokens/s per GPU (1.16×), with the same output distribution (579 vs 579 tokens per document, 8.5% vs 8.6% hitting the length cap; every metric of the distribution check within 3–11% of the steering effect). Each shard takes ~2.5× longer because three share a GPU, so jobs are sized at 36 shards per 3 nodes (one round, ~10–13 h). About a sixth fewer GPU-hours per corpus.

Sources: optimized_inference/README.md, throughput_sweep/results/SUMMARY.md, throughput_sweep/VALIDITY.md.

Caveats

  • One training seed per model. CIs on this page cover evaluation/item noise only.
  • α35 mixes also contain different human text (other CommonCrawl dumps, because longer synthetic documents shift which FineWeb shards fill the human half); this is collinear with α. Its measured effect on predictability is ~10× smaller than the synthetic difference, but it is not ruled out.
  • Source-document faithfulness concerns a document the model never sees during training.
